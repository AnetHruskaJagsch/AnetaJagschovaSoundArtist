Aneta Jagschová is a sound recordist, sound artist and educator. Her work has long revolved around absurdity, preservation and collecting; she struggles with the imperfection of her own memory and with the human need to gather and collect. She began developing her approach of sympathising with overlooked spaces and media in Vltavská už není tak cizí (Vltavská Is No Longer So Strange), a field-recording report on the non-place of Vltavská. In her diploma thesis Konzervování každodennosti (Preserving the Everyday) she explored preserving everyday objects as digital trophies in the context of open archives and participatory approaches to education. She has also studied and exhibited in Finland at Aalto University and in Porto at the Faculdade de Belas Artes da Universidade do Porto. Her interest in preservation then shifted from objects to the recording itself: today her practice moves between sound recording for documentary and fiction film and artistic research into the relationship between recorded sound, memory and listening, which she is developing in her master's studies at FAMU in Prague (Centre for Audiovisual Studies, Experimental Studio).
